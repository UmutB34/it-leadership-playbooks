# IT budget model: structure and logic

*A method, not a report. All figures below are illustrative and do not come from any organization.*

## Purpose

Give leadership a budget they can read in two minutes, defend line by line, and compare year over year, and give IT a way to explain cost in terms the business recognizes.

## Structure

Split every line two ways: **OPEX** (running costs) and **CAPEX** (investments), and by category:

| Category | What it contains | Typical share (illustrative) |
|---|---|---|
| Managed services | Provider fees for hosting, security operations, support | 30% |
| Licences and subscriptions | Productivity suite, security, ERP and database licences, maintenance | 28% |
| Hardware and refresh | Laptops, network devices, planned replacement | 15% |
| Projects | Approved initiatives with their own business case | 15% |
| People and training | Team development, certifications, contractors | 7% |
| Contingency | Reserve for unplanned work, held centrally | 5% |

## Logic

1. **Baseline first.** Last year's actuals plus contractual changes (price indexation, renewals, user counts).
2. **Drivers, not guesses.** Link lines to drivers: employees, devices, users, sites. A new hire adds a licence set and a device; a new site adds connectivity and support.
3. **Projects need a case.** Each project line carries purpose, cost, benefit, risk of not doing it, and an owner.
4. **Total cost over the contract period.** Compare vendors on the full term, not the year-one price.
5. **Contingency is explicit.** Hidden padding destroys trust; a visible reserve builds it.

## Unit economics worth tracking

- IT cost per employee.
- IT cost per device.
- Share of OPEX vs CAPEX.
- Cost of each major service per user (mail, endpoint, security, support).

## Negotiation levers

- Multi-year commitment for lower unit prices, with exit and price-cap clauses.
- Bundling services with one provider where it simplifies operations.
- An independent review of the infrastructure before renewal, so the request is based on need.
- A renewal calendar, so no contract auto-renews unseen.

## Cadence

- **Monthly:** actual vs budget by category, with variance explained in one line each.
- **Quarterly:** forecast update and project status.
- **Yearly:** next budget built from baseline, drivers and approved projects.

## What leadership sees

One page: total, OPEX/CAPEX split, cost per employee, top five cost drivers, projects with status, and the decisions needed.
