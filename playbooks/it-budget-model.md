# IT budget model: structure and logic

*This is a method, not a report. All figures below are illustrative and do not come from any organization.*

## Purpose

I want a budget that leadership can read in two minutes, that I can defend line by line, and that compares cleanly year over year. It also gives IT a way to explain cost in terms the business already understands.

## Structure

Split every line two ways: by OPEX (running costs) or CAPEX (investments), and by category.

| Category | What it contains | Typical share (illustrative) |
|---|---|---|
| Managed services | Provider fees for hosting, security operations, support | 30% |
| Licences and subscriptions | Productivity suite, security, ERP and database licences, maintenance | 28% |
| Hardware and refresh | Laptops, network devices, planned replacement | 15% |
| Projects | Approved initiatives with their own business case | 15% |
| People and training | Team development, certifications, contractors | 7% |
| Contingency | Reserve for unplanned work, held centrally | 5% |

## Logic

1. Start with the baseline. That is last year's actuals plus contractual changes such as price indexation, renewals and user counts.
2. Tie lines to drivers, not guesses. Drivers are employees, devices, users and sites. A new hire adds a licence set and a device. A new site adds connectivity and support.
3. Every project needs a case. Each project line carries its purpose, cost, benefit, the risk of not doing it, and an owner.
4. Compare vendors on total cost over the contract period, not the year-one price.
5. Show contingency openly. Hidden padding destroys trust, and a visible reserve builds it.

## Unit economics worth tracking

- IT cost per employee.
- IT cost per device.
- Share of OPEX vs CAPEX.
- Cost of each major service per user (mail, endpoint, security, support).

## Negotiation levers

- Multi-year commitment for lower unit prices, with exit and price-cap clauses.
- Bundling services with one provider where it simplifies operations.
- An independent review of the infrastructure before renewal, so the request is based on need.
- A renewal calendar, so no contract auto-renews unseen.

## Cadence

- Monthly: actual vs budget by category, with each variance explained in one line.
- Quarterly: forecast update and project status.
- Yearly: next budget built from the baseline, drivers and approved projects.

## What leadership sees

One page with the total, the OPEX/CAPEX split, cost per employee, the top five cost drivers, projects with status, and the decisions needed.
