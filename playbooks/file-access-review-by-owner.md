# File access review by business owner

*A method note from repeated annual reviews. No names, folders, counts or findings.*

## Why

IT can list who has access to a folder, but only the business can say who should. An auditor will ask for evidence that the people who own the data have confirmed it. The review is also the cleanest moment to remove leavers, role changes and one-off grants.

## Method

1. **Define owners.** Every top-level folder has a named owner in the business, usually the head of the unit. Record it.
2. **Export access per folder.** For each top-level folder (and, where needed, the next level) export who has what: read, modify, full control. Resolve groups to members. Remove technical accounts from the owner's view, but keep them in a separate sheet for IT.
3. **Send the owner only their own folders.** One file per owner, in plain columns: folder, person, current right, and an empty column "keep / change / remove". Do not send the whole file server.
4. **Set a deadline and a reminder.** Two weeks is usual. Track replies in a list with sent date, replied date and status.
5. **Apply the answers.** Change through groups, not individual grants. Keep the owner's reply as the change record.
6. **Handle corrections.** Owners often reply with a correction after the first answer; keep the latest dated reply and note that it supersedes the earlier one.
7. **Close with a summary** to the auditor and the owners: folders reviewed, replies received, changes made, exceptions with a reason and an expiry date.

## Rules that keep it honest

- No reply is not approval. Escalate to the owner's manager, then apply a defined default, for example read-only for the unit group.
- Privileged and shared accounts are reviewed by IT and security, not by unit owners.
- The review is repeated on a calendar, and also after any restructuring.
- Starters, movers and leavers are handled by their own process; the review catches what slipped through.

## Evidence

The owner list, the exports as sent, the replies, the change log, the exception list and the closing summary.
