# Vendor and supplier management

*A practical model for managing providers, resellers and service partners. Tool-neutral.*

## Goal

Get the service that was bought, at the price that was agreed, with risks understood, and keep the relationship useful for both sides.

## Lifecycle

1. **Need and market check.** Define the requirement first. Ask at least three providers for comparable offers.
2. **Evaluation.** Score on fit, total cost over the term, support model, exit terms and references. Weights are agreed before offers arrive.
3. **Contract.** Scope, service levels, response and resolution targets, reporting, price changes, data return and exit.
4. **Onboarding.** Named contacts on both sides, escalation path, access with least privilege and an end date.
5. **Operation.** Regular service review, SLA reporting, issue log, renewal calendar.
6. **Renewal or exit.** Decide with data: performance, cost, alternatives.

## Classify suppliers

| Tier | Description | Management intensity |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Failure stops the business (hosting, connectivity, ERP support) | Monthly review, tested exit plan |
| Important | Failure hurts but has a workaround | Quarterly review |
| Standard | Easily replaced | Annual check |

## Keep one register

For each supplier: service, owner, contract dates, renewal notice period, value, service levels, access granted, last review, risks. The register feeds the budget and the audit evidence.

## Good practice

- One internal owner per supplier, so there is always someone accountable.
- Third-party access is time-limited, named and logged.
- Service reports are checked against your own monitoring, not taken on trust.
- Price negotiations use total cost, volume and term, and are prepared with a market comparison.
- Keep notes of meetings and decisions; they settle later disagreements.

## Measures

- SLA attainment per supplier.
- Open issues and their age.
- Spend vs contract, and share of spend under contract.
- Number of renewals decided before the notice date.
